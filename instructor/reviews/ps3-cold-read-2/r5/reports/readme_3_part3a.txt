B047: The optimal production rate is unique, but the optimal flux vector often isn't. FVA finds the min and max of each flux over all optimal vectors, or over all vectors producing at least γ times the optimum. It cites the original FVA paper.
B048: γ is supply_ranges' `fraction` keyword. It must be between 0 and 1, and the default of 1 means only optimal solutions are considered.
B049: FVA here covers only the 20 supply fluxes, because internal forward/reverse pairs can cycle and give ranges that just reflect their bounds. Supply reactions can't cycle that way.
B050: Defines c as the protein objective and z⋆ as the maximum protein flux (in mM/h, not µM/h) for one strategy and budget, as a lead-in to the range problems.
B051: For each supply column, minimize and maximize its flux subject to the steady-state balance, bounds, the allocation inequality, and protein output ≥ γz⋆.
B052: The bounds, A, and b come from allocation_constraints for the same strategy and budget. Upcoming steps will show how to recast the min and ≥ problems for solve_lp, which only maximizes and only takes ≤ rows.
B053: A supply flux is "fixed" for reporting if its range width is ≤ 1e-5 mM/h. At γ=1 that means the optimum pins its value. supply_ranges returns only the endpoints, with no fixed flag.
